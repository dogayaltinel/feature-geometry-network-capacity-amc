\title{Feature Geometry Versus Network Capacity in Short-Window Automatic Modulation Classification}

\author{\uppercase{Dogay Altinel}\authorrefmark{1}, \IEEEmembership{Member, IEEE}}
\address[1]{Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Istanbul Medeniyet University, Istanbul 34700, T\"urkiye}
\corresp{Corresponding author: Dogay Altinel (e-mail: dogay.altinel@medeniyet.edu.tr).}
\tfootnote{This research has been supported by the Research Fund of Istanbul Medeniyet University. Project Number: 2025-GAP-MDBF-0005.}

\markboth{Altinel: Feature Geometry Versus Network Capacity}{Altinel: Feature Geometry Versus Network Capacity}

\begin{abstract}
Automatic modulation classification (AMC) accuracy depends on both the signal
representation and the capacity of the classifier, yet the two are usually
changed together, which makes reported gains hard to attribute. This paper
presents a controlled factorial study on RML2016.10a with 128-sample
observations. A nested feature geometry, from two-channel I/Q to a seven-channel
set with derived magnitude, unit-magnitude, and difference channels, is crossed
with the width of a single lightweight depthwise-separable backbone under one
training protocol, two data splits, and five seeds. Contrasts are predeclared,
paired at the split--seed level, and corrected for multiple comparisons, and test
data were accessed only after the analysis was locked. The first increase in
width gives a clear accuracy gain of about 1.6 percentage points for both
feature sets. Further widening gives no detectable gain for raw I/Q and a loss
for the seven-channel input, which also requires more operations at every width.
In this short-window setting, therefore, adding derived channels or enlarging
the network does not automatically improve accuracy. The findings are
conditional on one corpus, two splits, and one backbone, and do not establish
hardware or cross-corpus superiority. By separating representation from
capacity under a locked, reproducible protocol, the study provides a reference
for attributing accuracy gains in compact AMC models.
\end{abstract}
