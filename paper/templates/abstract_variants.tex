\begin{abstract}
Automatic modulation classification depends on both the signal representation
and the capacity of the classifier that processes it. This paper presents a
controlled short-window study that varies nested feature geometries and the
width of a common backbone while holding the corpus, observation length,
training schedule, and evaluation rules fixed. A factorial design, cost ledger,
matched-compute analysis, split replication, and a secondary-backbone control
are used to separate feature, capacity, and interaction effects. The results
will be reported without assuming in advance which factor is dominant and will
be interpreted within the declared data, window, and backbone scope.
\end{abstract}
